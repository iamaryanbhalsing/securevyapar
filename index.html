Identity & Payments
When you receive a UPI payment request from an unknown sender, what do you do?

This helps us understand your awareness of UPI scams.

Do you use the same password for your email and banking accounts?

Using the same password across sites increases risk if one is compromised.

How often do you back up your shop's important records (bills, customer data, etc.)?

Regular backups protect you from data loss.

Have you ever clicked on a link in a WhatsApp message offering "free recharge" or "gift"?

These are common scam patterns targeting shop owners.

Do you enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on your important accounts (email, banking)?

2FA adds an extra layer of protection beyond just a password.

When you receive an unexpected email about a "refund" or "payment failed", what do you do?

Phishing emails often impersonate banks or payment platforms.

How many devices (phone, tablet, computer) do you use to access your shop's billing or UPI apps?

More devices = more entry points for threats.

Have you installed security updates on your phone and apps in the last 30 days?

Outdated software contains known vulnerabilities.

Do you regularly check the URL before entering UPI PIN or OTP?

Scammers create fake UPI pages that look real.

Have you ever received a call from someone claiming to be from your bank asking for OTP or PIN?

Banks never ask for OTP or PIN over the phone.

Do you use a password manager or write down your passwords securely?

Reusing simple passwords across sites is a major risk.

How often do you review your bank and UPI transaction history?

Catching unauthorized transactions early reduces damage.

Have you ever scanned a QR code from an unknown source to make a payment?

Malicious QR codes can redirect payments to scammer accounts.

Do you use public Wi-Fi (at cafes, transit, etc.) to access your shop's billing or UPI apps?

Public networks can be intercepted if not using VPN or 2FA.

Are you aware of the most common UPI scams targeting shop owners in your area?

Awareness is the first line of defense.

Do you have a separate device or user account for shop staff to use for browsing or bills?

Separate accounts limit exposure if a staff member's device is compromised.

When you see a "Secure" (lock) icon in the browser address bar, what does it mean to you?

HTTPS means the connection is encrypted, but doesn't guarantee the site is safe.

Have you ever installed a "cracked" or "free premium" app from an unofficial source?

Such apps often contain malware or steal data.

Do you check app permissions before installing something new on your phone?

Apps requesting unrelated permissions (e.g., flashlight app asking for contacts) are suspicious.

Have you lost access to an account because of a forgotten password or locked device in the last year?

Loss of access can mean permanent loss of data or transactions.

Do you use the same browser on both your personal and shop devices?

Mixing personal and shop browsing can cross-contaminate cookies and saved passwords.

How do you handle links sent by unknown contacts on WhatsApp or Telegram?

Unknown links can lead to phishing sites or malware downloads.

Do you have an emergency plan if your shop's billing system is locked by ransomware?

Having a plan reduces downtime and data loss.

When you get a pop-up saying "Your shop is infected, click here to fix", what do you do?

These are fake alerts designed to scare you into downloading malware.
